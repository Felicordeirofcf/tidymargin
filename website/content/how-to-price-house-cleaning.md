# How to Price House Cleaning Jobs (Step by Step, With the Math)

Most pricing advice for cleaning businesses starts with "the average price in the US is...". Averages are a starting point for conversation, not for your business. Your pay rate, your speed, your drive times and your overhead are your own. This guide shows a method you can use for every job.

## Step 1: Estimate the labor hours

Labor hours are the total hours worked by everyone on the job. Two cleaners for 3 hours = 6 labor hours.

A simple model that works well:

- **Base time from square feet**: square feet / your square feet per labor hour.
- **Plus time per bedroom and per bathroom**: bathrooms usually take the longest.
- **Times a service multiplier**: a deep clean or move-out clean takes longer than a standard clean.
- **Times a condition multiplier** for pets or heavy use.

The only reliable source for these numbers is your own timing. Time a few jobs and write the numbers down.

## Step 2: Turn hours into cost

- **Labor** = labor hours x pay per hour x (1 + payroll burden %).
- **Supplies** for the job.
- **Travel** = round-trip miles x your cost per mile.
- **Overhead share** = monthly overhead / jobs per month.

Example with illustrative numbers: 6.9 labor hours at $18/hour with 12% burden is $139.36 of labor. Add $18 of supplies, $9.80 of travel and a $30 overhead share: the total cost is $197.16.

## Step 3: Price for a margin, not a markup

If you want to keep 30% of the price as profit and your card processor takes 2.9%, the price is:

> price = total cost / (1 - 0.30 - 0.029) = $197.16 / 0.671 = $293.82

Round up to a clean number such as $295. Adding 30% to the cost ($256.31) would leave you with a much smaller margin once the card fee is paid.

## Step 4: Apply discounts the right way

If you offer 10% off for every-2-weeks service, decide which number must stay profitable: the price the client actually pays. Then show a higher regular price and the discount. That way the discount comes out of the regular price, not out of your margin.

## Step 5: Check prices you already charge

Put your current recurring clients through the same math. Any client below your target margin is a candidate for a polite price increase with 30 days' notice.
