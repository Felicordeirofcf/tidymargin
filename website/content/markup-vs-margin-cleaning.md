# Markup vs Margin for Cleaning Businesses (and Why 30% Is Not 30%)

"I add 30% to my costs, so I make 30%." It is one of the most common pricing mistakes in service businesses, and it quietly costs money on every job.

## The two definitions

- **Markup** = profit / **cost**.
- **Margin** = profit / **price**.

Same profit, different base. Because the price is always bigger than the cost, the margin is always smaller than the markup.

## A cleaning example

A job costs you $100 (labor, supplies, travel and an overhead share).

- With a 30% markup you charge $130. Your profit is $30, which is **23.1%** of the price.
- To keep 30% of the price, you need to charge **$142.86**: $100 / (1 - 0.30).

That $12.86 difference on a single $100-cost job adds up quickly over a year of recurring cleans.

## Conversion table

| Margin you want | Markup needed |
|---|---|
| 10% | 11.1% |
| 20% | 25.0% |
| 25% | 33.3% |
| 30% | 42.9% |
| 35% | 53.8% |
| 40% | 66.7% |
| 50% | 100.0% |

Formula: markup = margin / (1 - margin). Margin = markup / (1 + markup).

## Do not forget the card fee

Card processors take a percentage of the amount you collect. If you want a 30% margin and pay a 2.9% fee, divide by (1 - 0.30 - 0.029), not by (1 - 0.30).

## Gross, contribution and net margin

- **Gross margin** subtracts labor and supplies only.
- **Contribution margin** also subtracts travel, card fees and other job costs.
- **Net margin** also subtracts the job's share of overhead (insurance, phone, software, marketing).

Net margin is the one that tells you what you keep before income taxes.
